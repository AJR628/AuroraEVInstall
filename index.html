Common questions

Quick answers before you commit

How much does an EV charger install cost in Aurora?

Based on Qmerit's 2025 installer-network data, the Colorado average is $2,398 with a typical range of $800–$3,000 before equipment. About 60% of Aurora homes only need a simple circuit addition at $500–$1,500. Equipment (Tesla Wall Connector, ChargePoint, etc.) is $300–$700 on top.

See our full Aurora EV Charger Cost Guide for the four tiers, six cost variables, and worked examples after incentives.

How long does the install take?

For most Aurora homes (Tier 1 — simple circuit addition), the install itself takes 4-6 hours on a single day. Includes permit pull, dedicated 240V circuit run, charger mounting, hardwiring or NEMA 14-50 install, and energizing. The Aurora final electrical inspection is scheduled separately, typically within a few days.

If you need a full service upgrade (Tier 4, 100A→200A), expect 2-4 weeks total due to Xcel utility coordination.

Is the federal tax credit still available?

Yes, but the deadline was shortened. Public Law 119-21 (July 2025) reduced the Section 30C termination date from December 31, 2032 to June 30, 2026. To claim the 30% credit (up to $1,000 per port), your charger must be installed and operational by that date.

There's also a census tract requirement — your home must be in a low-income community tract or a non-urban tract. Much of urban Aurora may not qualify. We'll help you check before you count on it. IRS source.

What about the Xcel Energy rebate?

Xcel Energy Colorado offers up to $500 standard for Level 2 charger wiring (or up to $1,300 enhanced for income-qualified customers). The catch: you must enroll in Xcel's Optimize Your Charge program before applying. Funding is limited and first-come, first-served.

Your electrician will walk you through the enrollment + application sequence as part of your install.

Do I really need a permit in Aurora?

Yes. Per Aurora City Code §22-213, any electrical work — including EV charger installation — requires an electrical permit pulled by a licensed contractor before work begins. Aurora inspects all EV charger installs to the 2023 National Electrical Code, which has been in effect since August 1, 2023.

Unpermitted work can void homeowner's insurance and cause issues when you sell. Source: Aurora Code of Ordinances §22-213.

Can I install it myself to save money?

No. Aurora City Code §22-213 requires that electrical permits for EV charger installation be pulled by a licensed electrical contractor. Homeowners cannot DIY a permitted electrical install in Aurora.

Working without a permit is technically possible but a very bad idea: it can void homeowner's insurance, fail safety inspections, cause issues when selling your home, and create real fire risk for chargers that draw 30-50 amps continuous.
